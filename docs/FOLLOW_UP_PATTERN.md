# The pattern: merge, protect, then prioritise

The same shape works for any event-driven follow-up (webinars, trade shows, product trials, content downloads). Three moves, always in this order.

## 1. Merge to one record per person before you judge anyone

Webinar exports are per registration and per join, not per person. If you score raw rows, someone who rejoined after a dropped connection looks like two half-engaged people. So the agent:

- normalises email (case, spaces) before anything else;
- sums join sessions and caps the total at the webinar length, so a host who joined early is not "120% engaged";
- tries a second key (name plus company) when an attendee's email is not a registrant, but holds that match for a human, because a wrong merge in the CRM is harder to undo than a missed one.

## 2. Protect before you prioritise

The segment rules run in a fixed order, and the first match wins:

1. **Quarantine** test, internal, competitor and invalid rows. They should never reach a list or a sequence.
2. **Suppress** anyone opted out of marketing, however engaged. The score is still calculated and visible, which is exactly why this rule must come before the hot band.
3. **Owner heads-up** for open deals and customers. Their owner already has the relationship; an SDR call or a nurture email from marketing would cut across it.
4. Only then do the **engagement bands** apply.

Most "the webinar nurture emailed my customer" incidents come from doing these in the other order.

## 3. Prioritise with reasons, and act on what they said

The score is simple on purpose: each point has a named reason that is printed next to it. Reps trust a list when they can see why someone is on it. The strongest signals are the ones the person chose to give you: a poll answer about timing and the question they typed. That is why the call opener quotes their question back to them rather than referring to "the webinar".

## What carries over

- One record per person, built from the messiest source first.
- Safety rules before scoring rules, in code, in a fixed order.
- Every automated judgement shows its reasons.
- Uncertain matches and policy edge cases go to a review queue instead of being guessed.
- The script prepares; humans and the CRM's own importer act.
